It is widely debated whether public funds should be directed primarily towards mass transit systems rather than highway infrastructure. In my view, while expanding and modernizing public transport deserves greater financial priority to alleviate urban issues, governments must continue to allocate sufficient resources to preserve essential road networks.

Directing a larger share of expenditure to public transportation offers immense economic and social benefits. Foremost among these is providing an economical alternative to personal vehicles. Operating a car entails substantial ongoing expenses, such as fuel, insurance, and vehicle maintenance, which place heavy financial burdens on lower-income citizens. Subsidized train and bus services ensure affordability for everyday commuters, thereby opening up vital access to jobs and education in major commercial districts. Furthermore, high-capacity transit networks encourage residents to leave private cars at home, leading to lower congestion on arterial city routes and reducing environmental emissions. Consequently, prioritizing public transit is fundamental for sustainable urban growth.

Nevertheless, neglecting road networks entirely would produce severe negative outcomes. First, maintaining vital roads is imperative because public transit vehicles, including regional buses and emergency ambulances, rely directly on well-paved surfaces. More critically, high-capacity mass transit systems such as subways are rarely economically feasible in sparsely populated areas. Therefore, continuous government investment is required to protect rural connections. Residents in agricultural belts depend on dependable roads to transport produce to markets and to access essential public services in neighboring towns. If road budgets are cut too drastically, remote communities risk being isolated economically and socially.

In conclusion, I largely agree that governments should focus their spending on expanding public transport systems, as this enhances commuter affordability, broadens employment opportunities, and mitigates traffic bottlenecks. However, funding for existing roadways must not be stripped away, because sound road infrastructure is indispensable for rural connectivity and general logistical stability.
